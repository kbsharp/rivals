Where each action should live

Two or three real designs for each problem area from the phase 2 audit, drawn in the brief's palette and type, with the scoreboard in landscape. Each option has a trade-off, the jobs it helps and hurts, and a recommendation. The storyboard at the end runs a whole night through the recommended options.

How to answer. Comment on an area with the option you want (1A, 3B…), a mix ("2A, but tags in the menu too"), or "drop this area". Tap any mock-up to see it full size.

All six areas are here. The audit asked you to pick which areas go forward, and no pick was recorded, so none were left out. Dropping an area is a perfectly good answer.

Jobs, by frequency: 1 record a frame · 2 undo · 3 tag · 4 end, start or change a match · 5 end the night · 6 start a night or glance · 7 history · 8 invite, sign in, quick game.

AREA 1

Fixing a mistake

F1 undo is silent on both phones · F2 it's two taps deep, next to End session · F3 nothing catches a double or stray frame · F13 the menu covers your rival's score. Job 2, a few times a night.

1A

The last frame, on the status line

Recommended

The status line gets one new thing: a receipt for the last frame. Undo is one tap, is always in the same place and never needs a precise tap on a menu row. What undo did is named on both phones. The receipt is also where tagging lives (2A), so one element serves jobs 2 and 3.

Helps
Job 2: one tap instead of two, with visible feedback. Job 1: you can see who scored the last frame. F3 is caught without slowing the tap.
Hurts
The board gets busier: one pill plus a button that's always there. The status line text moves right to make room.
1B

An undo toast after each frame

The familiar Android pattern. It's quiet between frames, and it confirms every frame on the other phone too.

Helps
Job 2 right after a wrong tap; the other phone sees each frame arrive.
Hurts
It flashes up dozens of times a night on the centre line, which the brief keeps empty. A mistake found a frame later is back to two taps in the menu. Doesn't help F3 unless you happen to be looking.
1C

Long-press a half to take it back

Nothing is added to the board. You press the score you think is wrong, and the panel tells you everything about that frame before anything changes.

Helps
Keeps the board clean. Undoing is deliberate, and whoever wants to check a frame can see who recorded it and when.
Hurts
Nobody finds it without being told. It only works if the last frame is that player's. A long press can land when the phone is picked up off the rail.
Recommendation · 1A

Undo is the second most common job, and 1A is the only option that makes it visible, one tap, and the same every time. The pill costs one element on the status line, and 2A uses it too. Undo also stays in the match-won panel as it is today.

AREA 2

Tagging

F5 tagging gives no feedback and doesn't say which frame, and the winning frame's tag is hidden behind the match-won panel. Job 3, a few times a night.

2A

Tap the last frame to tag it

Recommended

The tag goes on the frame it describes, in the place that already names that frame. There's no question of "which frame": the receipt says it.

Helps
Job 3: still two taps, but visible, labelled and seen on both phones. The winning frame gets tagged in the panel, where it happens.
Hurts
Only the last frame can be tagged on the board (the same as today). An older frame would need Session detail, which doesn't exist yet: a possible later addition.
2B

Long-press your half to win with a special

Scoring and tagging become one gesture, done at the moment it happens.

Helps
Job 3 without a second visit, and the tag can't go on the wrong frame.
Hurts
It's hidden. It puts a second meaning on the one gesture the board must never get wrong (job 1). If you forget and just tap, you still need another way to tag.
2C

Keep the tags in the menu, and say so

The smallest change: the same place, with the two gaps closed.

Helps
Fixes the feedback and "which frame" with almost no build.
Hurts
The menu keeps four jobs in it (F4), so area 3 can't be solved cleanly. The winning frame is still behind the panel.
Recommendation · 2A

It comes almost free with 1A and empties the menu of tags. The match-won panel is where the best tags happen, so the panel gets them.

AREA 3

The game menu

F4 the menu mixes four jobs and changes shape · F10 a wrong race can only be fixed by throwing the match away. Jobs 4, 5. With 1A and 2A, undo and tags have already left the menu.

3A

A short, fixed menu

Today's menu, pruned. Match settings opens today's Change game dialog, now allowed mid-match; a race can't be set at or below a score already on the board.

Helps
Grouping (F4) and F10, with the least new UI.
Hurts
Changing the race is still three taps plus a dialog. The menu still opens over your rival's score.
3B

A match sheet

Recommended

The match's settings and the ways out of it, in one place with big targets. It uses the same game chips and race stepper as the between-matches panel, so they look alike wherever you set a match. Changes apply at once, and the other phone's status line updates.

Helps
Job 4: a race is fixed in two taps with no dialog. F4 is solved, and every target is 44 dp or more. Nothing opens over the score in a corner.
Hurts
It's more to build than 3A. A sheet over the board is a bigger moment than a menu, but these are rare jobs.
3C

Tap what you want to change

Each action sits on the thing it changes: the match on the match line, and the night on the menu.

Helps
Grouping is as clean as it gets, and End session is on its own.
Hurts
Quiet text rarely looks tappable. It gives ≡ a single item. With 1A on the left and 6A making the line louder, the status line starts to crowd.
Recommendation · 3B

With undo and tags gone, what's left is "this match, and leaving". A sheet fits that better than a menu, and it fixes F10 properly instead of through a dialog. End session stays confirmed, as it is today.

AREA 4

Between matches

F8 the snackbar covers Start match · F10 the next match can't be set from the match-won panel · F12 three text actions of mixed kinds in a row. Job 4.

4A

The panel sets the next match

Recommended

Auto-start stays as it is. The panel just becomes the place to set the next match, and every message on the scoreboard uses the status line.

Helps
Job 4 at the natural moment. F8 and F12 are gone. Home and End session sit where they are on the board.
Hurts
The panel carries more (next match, tags, two buttons), so the 9 s timeout needs care: it stops once the panel is touched.
4B

Always stop between matches

There's one between-matches place, always the same, and the next match only starts when someone says so.

Helps
Consistency, and there's never a surprise about what the next match is.
Hurts
An extra tap for every match. It breaks two things the audit says must stay: the next match already running, and the panel that clears itself.
4C

Keep the panels, move things

A light fix: the same screens, re-ordered, plus "Change next match" as a text action in the match-won panel that opens today's dialog.

Helps
F8 and F12 with a small build.
Hurts
Still three mixed text actions, and messages still look different on the board and off it.
Recommendation · 4A

It keeps what works (auto-start, a panel that clears itself) and puts settings where the decision is made. The rule "messages live in the status line" also covers 1A's undo message and 2A's tag feedback, so there's one kind of message on the board.

AREA 5

Leaving and ending

F6 back goes two different places with no prompt · F7 resuming re-shows the match-won panel · F9 the night ends with no payoff, in different places. Jobs 5, 6, 7. In every option, resuming shows the board as you left it: "seen" is remembered per match (F7).

5A

Full time on the board

Recommended

Back: the arrow and the system back gesture both go Home, with no prompt; Resume session is one tap back. End: the full-time panel, then Done → Home, or See the night → Session detail.

Helps
Job 5 gets its payoff, in landscape, at the table, on both phones at once. Back means one thing. The night always ends in the same place (Home).
Hurts
An accidental edge swipe still leaves the board and turns the phone (though nothing is lost). Nights begun from Rivalry now end on Home.
5B

Ask before leaving, story after

This guards against the edge swipe on the rail. The payoff is the story that already exists in Session detail.

Helps
F6's accidental exit, and F9 with a screen that's already built.
Hurts
A prompt on back is unusual on Android, and it's a tap every time you meant it. The ending means turning the phone to portrait to read.
5C

End where the night lives

Back: as 5A. End: always Rivalry, with tonight's row open to its highlights. A quick game ends on Home's "On this phone" instead.

Helps
The night lands in its history (job 7), always in the same place for a rivalry.
Hurts
Two endings (rivalry or guest). It's portrait, and the payoff is a list row.
Recommendation · 5A

The end of the night deserves a moment at the table, not a trip to another screen. Home is the one place every night can end. A prompt on back would cost a tap every time it's meant, to protect a mistake that loses nothing.

AREA 6

Glancing from the rail

F11 the status line can't be read from a table's length; the brief's open question about telling the halves apart in a bright room. Jobs 1, 4.

6A

A louder status line

Recommended

The same line and the same place, one step louder, with tonight's score shown as a score. The race is already on the board as pips.

Helps
Tonight's score and the match can be read from a few metres away.
Hurts
It's a new size on the line (15 isn't a brief step; it goes in the brief if chosen), and it takes a little of the board's quiet.
6B

Tonight's matches over each name

Tonight's score moves from text to a shape on each half, readable without reading.

Helps
You see who's winning the night at a glance, from anywhere.
Hurts
It's a new element on the board, next to the pips, which are also counts. The match, game and race are still quiet.
6C

Tinted halves

Answers "whose half is whose" in a bright room.

Helps
You can tell the halves apart at any distance and in any light.
Hurts
Doesn't touch F11. It adds colour to a board the brief keeps charcoal. It can only be judged on the phone.
Recommendation · 6A, and try 6C in phase 5

6A fixes the finding. Whether 6C is needed is a question about real light, so leave the brief's open question open and try the tint during the real night in phase 5.

FOR THE BRIEF

Rules of thumb these would add

Once the decisions are in, these go in design/brief.md and the Session section of CLAUDE.md, so later changes keep to them.

  • An action lives on the thing it acts on.About the last frame (undo, tags): on the last frame's receipt. About the match: the match sheet, or the match-won panel for the next one. About the night: last in the sheet.
  • The other phone is never surprised.Anything done to the score (a frame, an undo, a tag) is named on both phones, and says which phone did it when it wasn't yours.
  • Messages on the scoreboard go in the status line.Never over a button, and never a snackbar outside the palette.
  • Back from the scoreboard goes Home, however you do it.Leaving never ends the night. Resuming shows the board as you left it.
  • A night ends at the table.A full-time panel on both phones, then Home.
