# Rivals privacy policy

_Last updated: 22 September 2026_

Rivals is an app for keeping pool scores between friends, made by Kevin Bevan. You can keep score without an account; signing in with Google lets you set up a rivalry with a friend and keep your games together.

## What the app stores

When you sign in with Google, Rivals stores:

- **Your Google account name, email address and profile photo link**, so the app can show who's playing. Your email address is also indexed so a friend who types your exact address can invite you; nobody can browse or search the list of players.
- **Quick games played without signing in** stay on your phone only, until you choose to save one to a rivalry.
- **An account ID** that Firebase assigns to your sign-in.
- **The games you record**: sessions (nights out), matches, frames, who won and who broke each frame, the date and time, and a venue name if you enter one.

If the app crashes, **Firebase Crashlytics** receives a crash report: the error, your device model and Android version, and a random installation ID. It doesn't include your name, email or scores.

## Where it's stored and who can see it

Everything is stored in Google Firebase (Firebase Authentication, Cloud Firestore and Crashlytics), which Google runs on Rivals' behalf. Data is encrypted in transit.

A rivalry's scores can only be read or changed by its two players. Someone who knows your exact email address can see your name and profile photo, to invite you. Nothing is sold, shared with anyone else, or used for advertising. The app has no ads and no analytics beyond crash reports.

## Keeping and deleting data

Scores are kept until they're deleted. You can delete any past session in the app (History, then open the session and tap Delete). To have your account and everything linked to it deleted, email <CONTACT EMAIL> and it will be done within 30 days. Crash reports are kept by Crashlytics for 90 days.
